# First UART reception

2026, 14:17:25–14:18:06 America/Lima. The owner reports connecting four contacts to a YP-01 USB-TTL adapter and confirms 5 V/GND power according to the reference. macOS identifies Prolific Technology, VID:PID 067b:2303, port `/dev/cu.usbserial-113120`.

The port was opened at 8N1, without flow control, with DTR/RTS requested off. No byte and no BREAK were transmitted. Opening the port configures the adapter's lines; this does not mean its TX is electrically disconnected. The port was closed at the end.

[Raw events](../evidence/uart-2026-11-18/reception-original.jsonl). There was no intentional reset of the board, no loopback test and no physical change of wires during this acquisition. The result only shows an absence of bytes in those windows: it does not confirm that UART is disabled, that the wiring is correct, or a hardware failure.

After the test, the owner stated «5v, usb to ttl yp-01». There is no TX measurement and no verifiable specification of that particular module: this information does not allow USB power and the TX logic level to be told apart with certainty. No commands will be sent until logic compatibility is confirmed. External 5 V power does not make the MCU signals 5 V tolerant. See [hardware identification](../hardware.md).

A second, receive-only test was proposed: with power cut, disconnect the adapter's TX and move only RX to the other signal contact; keep the identified power and ground and power up again. At that point it was pending physical confirmation from the owner. This avoids connecting two possible outputs to each other and allows listening on the other contact without transmitting from the adapter. The later acquisition after the reported swap is documented below.

The UART precedent uses EstiUART and requires that mode to be enabled first; there is no evidence of an AT or free-text console on this unit. [Source of the precedent](https://mlusiak.com/estimoge-uart-raspberry-pi/). Silence alone does not allow concluding that UART is disabled.

## Second reception after the reported swap

The owner replied "I've swapped them, check". They did not confirm that the adapter's TX was disconnected: that uncertainty is kept in the record. Only reception was performed, with no bytes transmitted and no BREAK.

2026 14:20:05–14:20:46 America/Lima: 115200 and 9600 baud were repeated for 8 s each; 57600, 38400, 19200 and 230400 for 6 s each; all windows at 8N1 received **zero bytes**. The port was closed at the end. [Swapped reception log](../evidence/uart-2026-11-18/reception-inverted.jsonl).

During this reception, eight BLE advertisements with the same short Estimote identifier were received, confirming radio activity in that window. [BLE check](../evidence/uart-2026-11-18/ble-after-swap.json). It does not prove absence of damage, correct current draw or a working UART.

Swapping the contacts produced no observable UART activity. The hypotheses of a disabled UART mode, no spontaneous output, wiring/contact problems or an unvalidated adapter remain open. No requests will be sent until the logic levels are checked. To separate adapter problems from beacon problems, the next useful check would be a loopback of the USB-TTL isolated from the board; for the unit, the adapter's RX connected to one signal at a time with the adapter's TX disconnected, or a signal measurement with instruments.

## Final contribution from the owner

The owner reports having also tried on another laptop with 3.3 V and 5 V, without success. No logs, baud rates or diagram were provided, and it is not clear whether those voltages refer to power or to logic levels. It is recorded as a reported observation, not as proof of electrical compatibility.

Their hypothesis is a lock that would require an unlock command that is no longer accessible. The historical precedent makes it plausible that UART is not enabled in the firmware, but it does not confirm a physical lock or a command applicable directly over serial. The current impossibility of authenticated access via SDK/cloud was not checked either. SWD protection was not measured; it is independent of the GPIO/UART mode.
