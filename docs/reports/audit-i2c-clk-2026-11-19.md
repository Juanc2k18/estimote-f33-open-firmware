# Audit · origin of the I²C/SCL and of the clock · 2026

Documentary review of `docs/status.md`, the logbook, evidence r16–r30,
`sensors-session-01`, `scripts/board_diagnostics.py` and the UWB teardown
(DIY Stuff, consulted again today). **No physical tests were run, the board was
not touched and firmware/UICR were not changed.** Everything below about causes is
**inference** that has to be confirmed with measurements.

## 1. What is already firm

- MCU, SWD (A/B), UART (E1/E2), reset (G), SWO (E), VDD (C) and GND (F) confirmed.
- Components by marking: M41T62 (I²C RTC 0x68, integrated crystal, SQW at
  32 kHz at power-up), BMP280 very likely and FEM SKY66110-11.
- RTC6 (IRQ/OUT) → P0.19, according to the owner. SDA (RTC1) and SCL (RTC8) are still untraced.
- **Useful precedent:** the original telemetry from 18 Nov flagged a *clock error*
  and gave plausible pressure, light (322–346 lx), temperature and acceleration. Therefore,
  with the Estimote firmware **the sensors did respond**. Most likely the
  RTC had the OF oscillator-failure bit set after losing power. In other words,
  the bus worked and the problem lies in how we looked for it, not in dead sensors.

## 2. Key reinterpretation: 6/7/14/15 look like a power rail, not a bus

Data already recorded:

| Observation | Source |
| --- | --- |
| P0.14 **or** P0.15 high drives P0.06 **and** P0.07 high, even with pull-down | r16 |
| P0.06 high takes P0.07 from 0 to 1 | r25, enables matrix |
| With 14 or 15 high, neither 6 nor 7 goes low when driven OD for 50 ms | r24, r25 |
| With 14/15 in Z, 6/7 stay at 0 even with the ~13 kΩ internal pull-up | r24 |

An I²C pull-up resistor (2.2 to 10 kΩ) **does give way** when the nRF pulls
the line low. That it does not give way indicates a low-impedance node common to 6, 7, 14 and 15.
What fits is **four GPIOs in parallel powering a switched rail**
(VDD_SENS). This is a common technique in low-power beacons. With the rail
off, its load (sensors, capacitors, a possible divider or the light sensor)
holds it at 0 against the internal pull-up.

Consequence: **6/7 were never the I²C bus**. The attempts in r16–r25 on that
pair were bound to get no response, which matches what was observed.

## 3. Why the bus did not show up: a method bias

With the rail off, the SDA/SCL lines of an unpowered sensor are clamped
to ~0 V by its ESD diodes. The same happens if the external pull-ups hang
from that rail while it is off. Then **the GPIO does not recover the high level with the internal pull-up**
and the OD matrix marks it as invalid.

- The matrix was run **without the rail**: in r25 and r29 in ZZ, and in `sensors-session-01`
  with `enable: null`. The HZ/ZH states of r29 were only applied **afterwards**, to the
  19 pins that had already passed in ZZ.
- The pins that failed or stayed stuck low (**3, 4, 5, 11, 22, 25**, plus
  the coupled 9/10) **were not tested as I²C with the rail on**.
  `board_diagnostics.py` has them in `RESERVED` and always excludes them.
- 25 passed in r25/r29 but was stuck low in r16. That inconsistency between
  power conditions is another clue.
- The full rail was never turned on: 14 and 15 at the same time (plus 6/7) high with
  *high drive*. The rule «never 14 and 15 high» came from assuming they were the FEM's
  CTX/CRX. That assumption **contradicts** the effect on 6/7: a FEM control
  line does not power two other GPIOs.
- The wait time was 150 ms to 1 s, which is enough for BMP280 and
  LIS2DH12 startup. What was missing was the rail, not the time.

Conclusion: **the I²C is very likely among the excluded pins (3, 4, 5,
11, 22, 25, perhaps 9/10) and is only valid with the rail on.** Your intuition
that the connection is not direct fits: the bus depends on a power supply switched
by GPIO, and perhaps on switches or MOSFETs (see §5).

## 4. Origin of the clock (CLK)

**a) Bus SCL.** It is generated by the nRF. The real question is which GPIO
RTC8/SCL ends at. So far there is only continuity for RTC6 → P0.19.

**b) 32 kHz clock (LFCLK).** «32 kHz crystal present» was deduced only
because LFXO starts (LFCLKSTAT 0x10001). **The crystal has not been seen or
measured.** There are two options:

1. Discrete crystal on XL1/XL2 (P0.00/P0.01). The UWB I1.2 fits one (3215,
   32.768 kHz, 9 pF). On the F3.3 the small metal package «A728L», next to the
   nRF, is a candidate.
2. The M41T62's SQW (32 kHz from power-up) entering via XL1. LFXO would also
   start that way.

No test distinguishes between the two yet. Knowing it would also give a
**proof of life for the RTC without I²C**.

**c) Bootloader pins.** It configures 17/19 as LEDs and 20/22 as buttons with
pull-up. 19 is the RTC IRQ and 22 is stuck low: this binary conditions
the state of those pins at every boot.

## 5. Indirect connection? What the precedents say

- UWB I1.2 (DIY Stuff): the author does not publish the sensor mapping, only that the
  BMP280 shares SDA/SCL with «other I²C components». The author does document indirection
  in other blocks: TXB0104/TXS0101 level translators towards the DW1000, with
  *enables* from GPIO, a switched section rail, the SiP32101 battery
  switch and a CSD23203W P-MOSFET. Its battery divider goes to AIN2; on
  the F3.3 it would be AIN3/P0.05, and r20 supports this.
- In the r28 photo, the area next to the connector and the RTC has several SOT-23s and
  inductors: transistors, switches or a regulator. They cannot be identified
  without marking.
- Hypotheses ordered by probability:
  1. Sensors and pull-ups on the 6/7/14/15 rail, and SDA/SCL direct or with
     series resistors to other GPIOs.
  2. An always-on rail for the RTC, separate from the sensors. VDD is 2 V
     when powered via H: a regulator with selectable output.
  3. MOSFET or *buffer* isolation between bus branches (BSS138 style, or
     a load switch with active-low enable).

  The F3.3 has no DW1000, so the UWB's TXB0104 does not carry over.

## 6. Omitted or incomplete tests

Via SWD, without touching flash or UICR:

1. **Turn on the full rail:** 6, 7, 14 and 15 as high outputs at the same time with
   H0H1. Wait 50 ms and **repeat the OD matrix on all pins**, including
   3, 4, 5, 11, 22 and 25. Then do targeted I²C (0x18/19, 0x50–0x57, 0x68,
   0x76/77) on the pins that **become valid**. This is the most cost-effective test.
   Before that, confirm with the owner that there is no other contact that could clash.
2. **Compare the matrix with the rail off and on:** the pins that change from
   invalid to valid are the candidates for SDA/SCL, INT or CS.
3. **Frequency counter per GPIO** (GPIOTE + TIMER in RAM, 100 ms per pin)
   on all pins, with the rail off and on. If ~32 768 Hz appears,
   that pin is the RTC's SQW, which locates RTC2 and proves the RTC is
   powered. No current tool counts edges.
4. **Discriminate the LFCLK:** stop LFCLK, release P0.00/P0.01 as inputs and
   count edges. A passive crystal gives no edges; the SQW does. Alternative:
   `LFCLKSRC` with BYPASS+EXTERNAL and check whether RTC0 advances.
5. **Active-low enables:** test each free GPIO as a **low** output (not
   only high) with the rail on. This detects a load P-MOSFET.
6. **GPIO11 with current limiting:** SWD is lost when driving it, in two campaigns
   and while powered via H. It could control the regulator or a power
   switch. Test it powering directly via C (the regulator does not
   take part) and measuring C/F during the test.
7. **Bootloader bias:** run the tests after halting the core before it
   configures 17/19/20/22, or recompile `board.h` without an LED on 19.
8. **Analog reading of 3/4** (AIN1/AIN2) with the rail on, alternating
   covered and flashlight. The light sensor may be analog (a phototransistor) and only
   work with the rail.

Physical, with power supply and USB removed:

9. **Continuity RTC1/SDA and RTC8/SCL** to the nRF pins, starting
   with those corresponding to GPIOs 3/4/5/11/22/25. Measure in ohms: 0–100 Ω
   indicates a direct connection or a series resistor; hundreds of ohms or more
   point to a MOSFET or *buffer* in between.
10. **RTC1/RTC8 and RTC5 against 6/7/14/15:** if the pull-ups (2–10 kΩ) lead to the
    rail, that confirms §2. Also check RTC3 → F to fix the orientation.
11. **Marking of the SOT-23s and of the chip with a *datamatrix* next to the RTC**, and a
    photo of the **back side**, which has not been taken yet.
12. If an oscilloscope or logic analyzer is available, look at RTC2/SQW and XL1 at the same time.

## 7. Recommendation

Start with test 1, with test 3 in parallel. If, with the rail on, any excluded pin
becomes valid and responds at 0x68 or 0x76, the bus is located without
further continuity work. If it does not respond, continuity tests 9/10 decide whether there is an intermediate component.

## 8. Tool prepared: `scripts/rail_survey.py` (not yet run on the board)

Implements tests 1–4 of §6. Software tests: `tests/test_rail_survey.py`
(9 cases on a register-level simulated nRF52832). They check the single/split
topology, the reservations, the counter control, that 3/4 appear as
new pins only with the rail, that an I²C with no device gives NACK and not a failure,
and the distinction between a passive crystal and a square wave on XL1. They do not replace
physical validation.

Sequence, from a loop in RAM. It does not flash or write UICR and resets at the end,
reusing `ActiveBoard`:

1. **Positive counter control:** GPIOTE→PPI→TIMER1. Alternates pull-up and
   pull-down on GPIO19 (or 18) and requires exactly N rising edges. If it fails, it aborts: the
   zeros would not be interpretable.
2. **Topology:** drives each pin of 6/7/14/15 high separately, with standard
   drive and the others with pull-down. **It only turns on the full rail if the
   other three follow the pin in all four cases.** That way, driving all four high
   does not change the electrical node and avoids the CTX=CRX=1 condition if 14/15 were not
   joined.
3. **Rail off:** counts edges on 23 GPIOs as inputs (includes 11 and 19,
   read only). Tests the LFCLK: with LFCLK stopped, XL1/XL2 as GPIO, then
   crystal mode and BYPASS+EXTERNAL mode, measuring RTC2 against the host clock.
   Reads SAADC for VDD and AIN0–7, including P0.03/04. Runs the OD matrix on 21 pins.
4. **Rail on:** drives 6/7/14/15 high with H0H1 and verifies they reach
   high. Repeats step 3 and computes the difference between matrices. Then runs I²C
   (17 addresses: accelerometer, light, 0x50–0x57 EEPROM, RTC 0x68, BMP280) only
   on pairs with at least one **new** pin; `--all-pairs` extends it to all. At the end
   it checks that the rail stayed high and turns it off.

Exclusions: 12/13 (UART to the Pico) and 21 (nRESET) are not touched. 0/1/19 only as
inputs. GPIO11 is not included in the matrix without `--include-11`, because it lost SWD in
r25/r29.

Before running: stop the UART monitor, confirm the setup (for example,
3.3 V on C/F) and pass it in `--supply`.

```bash
python3 scripts/rail_survey.py --plan
```

```bash
python3 scripts/rail_survey.py --supply "3,3 V C/F; H/E3 sueltos" --out docs/evidence/rail-r31-2026-11-19
```

Output: `events.jsonl`, `summary.json`, `report.md` (with the section
«Riel, frecuencia y LFCLK») and `rail_summary.json`.

How to read the result:

- **single node = False**: the rail does not turn on. Check which pins follow
  which before adjusting the selection.
- **~32 768 Hz on some GPIO:** that pin is the RTC's SQW (RTC2) and the RTC is
  powered. If it only appears with the rail, the RTC hangs off the rail.
- **LFCLK «externa»** (external): the nRF's 32 kHz comes from a square wave,
  probably the SQW. «Cristal pasivo» (passive crystal): there is a dedicated crystal.
- **New pins with the rail plus validated ACK:** that is the bus. Repeat with the
  targeted pair in `board_diagnostics.py --i2c SDA:SCL` to read the ID or the
  calendar.
- **No new pins:** the bus does not depend on this rail, or there is an intermediate
  component. Move on to continuity (§6.9–10) and active-low enables (§6.5).
