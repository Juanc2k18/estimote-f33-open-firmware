## r12 · First SWD connection (read-only)

**Goal:** validate the SWD link via A/B and read the protection state, without erasing.

**Connections (owner):** Pico GP2 (pin 4) → A (SWDCLK), GP3 (pin 5) → B (SWDIO), GND (pin 3) → F. Beacon powered from an external 3.3 V power supply on C / GND on F; Pico 3V3 pin not connected.

**Tools:** Raspberry Pi Pico (RP2040) with debugprobe v2.3.1 (`debugprobe_on_pico.uf2`, SHA-256 `6649ebba11df46cfa6cfa1faec9706bd21303893cf3f1e40067284bd4e3792d4`); pyOCD 0.46.1 (`python3 -m pyocd`), target `nrf52832`, 1 MHz, `auto_unlock=false`.

**Result (measured):**
- DP IDR = `0x2BA01477` (Arm SW-DP, v1 rev2): **the SWD link via A/B works**.
- AP#0 IDR = `0x23000000` (AHB-AP, no access), AP#1 IDR = `0x02880000` (**Nordic CTRL-AP**): confirms an nRF52.
- pyOCD: "APPROTECT enabled: not automatically unlocking". **The chip is protected**: the flash cannot be read and the Estimote firmware cannot be backed up over SWD.
- Nothing was erased.

**Next:** the only path via SWD is a full erase through CTRL-AP (`python3 -m pyocd erase -t nrf52832 --mass`), which removes the original firmware and configuration. Explicit authorization is required before this critical step.

## r13 · Full erase and unlock

**Goal:** remove APPROTECT so the nRF can be programmed. **The original Estimote firmware was lost**: no backup was possible (r12).

**Connections and tools:** those of r12 (Pico debugprobe v2.3.1, pyOCD 0.46.1, 1 MHz, external 3.3 V power supply on C/F).

**Process:**
1. `python3 -m pyocd erase -t nrf52832 -f 1000000 --mass -v` → pyOCD wrote ERASEALL through CTRL-AP and then reported "Mass erase timeout waiting for ERASEALLSTATUS / unable to unlock device".
2. Direct reading of CTRL-AP (`scripts/nrf_ctrlap.py`): ERASEALL=1 (pyOCD had not cleared it), ERASEALLSTATUS=0, **APPROTECTSTATUS=1 (unprotected)**. The erase had in fact completed; the pyOCD error was a false negative.
3. `scripts/nrf_ctrlap.py clear`: ERASEALL=0 and reset pulse through CTRL-AP. APPROTECTSTATUS stays at 1.

**Result (measured):**
- FICR INFO.PART `0x10000100` = `00052832`; INFO.VARIANT `0x10000104` = `41414230` (**"AAB0"**: QFAA, revision B0); INFO.RAM `0x1000010C` = `0x40` (64 KB).
- UICR APPROTECT `0x10001208` = `0xFFFFFFFF` (no protection); PSELRESET `0x10001200` = `0xFFFFFFFF`; CUSTOMER `0x10001080` blank.
- Flash from `0x00000000` = `0xFFFFFFFF`: **chip blank and accessible over SWD**.

**Next:** choose and flash firmware (e.g. Adafruit bootloader to work over E1/E2, Espruino, or custom firmware with nRF Connect SDK/Arduino nRF5).

## r16 · Sensor search over SWD (partial)

**Goal:** locate sensors, buses and power before writing a BLE application. All over SWD with the core halted; no flash changes.

**New tools:** `scripts/gpio_survey.py` (levels with no pull / pull-down / pull-up), `scripts/gpio_power_probe.py` (each pin as output high/low and which others rise), `scripts/i2c_scan_swd.py` (TWI0 via registers) and `scripts/i2c_bitbang_swd.py` (I2C via GPIO, independent of peripherals).

**Results (measured):**
- **32 kHz crystal present:** LFXO starts in ~400 ms (LFCLKSTAT `0x10001`). Valid for the SoftDevice.
- **nRF internal temperature sensor:** 32.5 °C read over SWD (chip temperature, uncalibrated).
- **P0.14 / P0.15:** setting either HIGH leaves P0.06 and P0.07 high even with pull-down. Provisional interpretation: P0.14/P0.15 power a network with pull-ups toward P0.06/P0.07 (sensor power via GPIO).
- **P0.06 / P0.07:** with P0.14/P0.15 high, both stuck high. **No I2C device responds** in 0x08–0x77 in either order (TWI and bit-bang). Alternative hypothesis: SPI chip-selects with pull-up.
- **P0.03, P0.04, P0.11, P0.22, P0.25:** stuck low even with internal pull-up, with and without P0.14/P0.15. Consistent with push-pull outputs idling low (e.g. accelerometer interrupts) or with GND.
- **P0.09 / P0.10:** tied to each other at low resistance (setting one high raises the other). Consistent with an **NFC coil** between them, although the owner does not see one.
- **P0.17:** probable LED (bootloader blinking).
- Rest: floating.
- The TWI peripheral driven from the debugger generated no events; bit-bang did drive the lines. Why remains to be understood.

**Pending:** identify the sensor bus (SPI with CS on P0.06/P0.07, or another I2C pair). The original firmware published temperature, pressure, light and acceleration (status.md), so those sensors exist.

### r16 (cont.) · Native RAM scanner

A scanner was written in C that is loaded into RAM over SWD and runs at native speed ([tools/ram-scanner](../../tools/ram-scanner/README.md)). The flash is not touched.

**Results (measured):**
- **SPI, power P0.14/P0.15 high, CS on P0.06/P0.07:** 17,640 combinations of SCK/MOSI/MISO over 21 free pins, 4- and 3-wire, modes 0 and 3, 5 WHO_AM_I commands. **No response** other than 0x00/0xFF.
- **I2C with real external pull-up:** with P0.14 and P0.15 high, the only pair is P0.06/P0.07, and **nobody responds**. With only one of the two high, ACK on every address: false positive due to weak pull-up.
- **With P0.06/P0.07 high as power** and I2C on the rest, including P0.14/P0.15: nothing.
- **Cross probing with P0.14/P0.15 high:** setting any other pin high or low does not change any other pin.

**Interpretation:** P0.14 and P0.15 power, probably in parallel, a network with resistors toward P0.06/P0.07. No responding sensor was found on the buses tested. Other identification commands, another enable, or an analog sensor (light on a SAADC input) may be missing.

**Proposed next step:** macro photography of the board's chips to identify the sensors by their marking, and continuity from their pins to the nRF. This is more effective than continuing blindly.
